Software architecture is not just about structuring systems — it’s about making intentional decisions that balance business needs, technical constraints, and long-term sustainability. As systems evolve toward cloud-native and AI-driven paradigms, architects must expand their perspective beyond traditional design concerns.

> **Key insight:** Key insight: Modern architecture is no longer static. It is adaptive, data-driven, and increasingly influenced by AI capabilities — requiring architects to think in layers of abstraction and uncertainty.

## Software Architecture Guide

Hello everyone! I'm sharing this Guide to Software Architecture, an interactive resource I designed to guide you from essential fundamentals to the most advanced AI architectures.

The guide is structured in 7 chapters covering:

- **Fundamentals** — Software architecture is defined not just as a diagram, but as the set of significant decisions that are costly to change and serve as the bridge between business requirements and technical implementation.
- **Architecture Styles** — While the industry has moved toward microservices, a well-designed Modular Monolith is still a valid choice for small teams, provided there is clear logical separation between modules.
- **Quality Attributes** — These are measurable properties (such as scalability, security, and performance) that determine how well a system satisfies its stakeholders, and their analysis is the primary driver of design trade-offs.
- **Cross-Cutting Concerns** — Modern observability is built on the "Three Pillars" (Logs, Metrics, and Traces), which are increasingly unified by OpenTelemetry as the standard for instrumentation.
- **Data Architecture** — Choosing the right database is a fundamental architectural decision; for example, Vector Databases are essential for GenAI as they store high-dimensional embeddings for semantic similarity searches.
- **Cloud Architecture** — A key paradigm is Event-Driven Architecture (EDA), where producers emit events without knowing the consumers, allowing for total decoupling in both time and space.
- **AI Architectures** — The guide highlights RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), a technique that combines a model's generative power with dynamic information retrieval to provide grounded and citable responses.

It's a tool designed for architects looking to master current and future technological challenges.
